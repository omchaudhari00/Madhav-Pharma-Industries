# Editorial Functional Beverage

Design an original consumer-product site that feels like a smart editorial
publication with a commerce layer. It should be visually quiet, scientifically
legible, and unusually confident about restraint.

## Originality boundary

Do not recreate the STILL wordmark, numbered can artwork, product photography,
formula, ingredients, pricing, timeline, stockists, or phrases. Use the new
brand's real product information, packaging, and medical/legal approvals.

## Visual direction

- Use a warm off-white or pale mineral ground, ink-black typography, and one or
  two muted product accents chosen from the new brand palette.
- Pair an assertive neo-grotesk display face with a restrained serif italic for
  emotional emphasis. Use the serif sparingly.
- Make a single product object the hero. Oversized type can move behind or around
  it, but product legibility must survive on small screens.
- Use generous white space, hairline dividers, compact labels, and measured data
  tables. The visual language should feel like packaging specifications.
- Keep the header compact: wordmark, anchor links, a shop route, and cart count.

## Page architecture

1. **Formula hero** — one clear promise, product object, a compact proof line
   (only if accurate), and a scroll invitation.
2. **Flavour/product range** — a numbered carousel or selector with one active
   SKU at a time: flavor, moment of use, short description, and key inputs.
3. **Ingredients inside** — an accessible tab interface. Each tab shows source,
   role, amount, and a plain-English explanation; never imply clinical proof
   beyond what the brand can substantiate.
4. **Brand story** — an editorial year-by-year timeline with original archival
   images and narrative captions.
5. **Press or cultural proof** — editorial pull quotes, awards, or partnerships
   with real attribution. Avoid fabricated magazine logos and quotations.
6. **Find / buy** — stockist city switcher, direct product cards, pack choice,
   subscription option if real, newsletter, and a cart drawer.
7. **Footer** — concise mission, product links, terms, privacy, and contact.

## Interaction and motion

- A pinned hero or ingredient panel can be powerful, but it must release cleanly
  and preserve normal page scroll for keyboard and reduced-motion users.
- Rotate or float a product only when a real 3D model or high-quality render is
  available. Otherwise use layered 2D images and an honest static fallback.
- Product selectors, ingredient tabs, timeline years, city switches, pack
  options, cart, and newsletter need visible selected/disabled/error states.
- Use 150–350 ms UI transitions. Favor opacity and transforms; do not make the
  cart or product picker feel theatrical.
- Custom cursor effects are optional and must not replace regular pointer,
  focus, or touch feedback.

## Build guidance

- Treat add-to-cart as production functionality: quantity, price, availability,
  feedback, and checkout handoff must be real or clearly marked as a demo.
- Use semantic tabs, labels, button text, live messaging for cart updates, and
  keyboard-operable carousels.
- Optimize product images, pre-size media to prevent layout shift, and preserve
  color contrast on muted backgrounds.
- Keep product and ingredient information in structured data so content remains
  consistent across product cards, cart, and detail sections.

## Delivery checklist

- The brand looks composed without relying on the source site's packaging.
- Commerce works without scroll effects or a custom cursor.
- Ingredient claims, dosage, and testimonials are real and appropriately
  qualified.
- The page is calm, data-aware, and easy to scan at desktop and mobile sizes.
