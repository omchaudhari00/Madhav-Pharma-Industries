# Expressive Creative Portfolio

Create a personal portfolio that feels authored rather than templated. Let one
strong visual metaphor carry the experience, then back it up with precise work,
capability, process, and contact information.

## Originality boundary

Do not copy the reference creator's face, photography, ornamental graphics,
logo, red-and-black composition, word fragments, manifesto, social handles,
work images, or process copy. Develop a new visual concept from the creator's
own identity, original assets, and actual portfolio.

## Visual direction

- Choose one memorable art direction, such as chromatic poster collage, soft
  biomorphic 3D, neon technical drafting, analog photography, or a palette from
  the creator's work. Do not default to a derivative red/black palette.
- Combine an oversized display face with a clear, small utility sans. Allow type
  to crop and span screens, but keep every important sentence readable.
- Use a human portrait, avatar, or abstract signature asset only when licensed
  and representative of the creator. A visual motif should feel personal, not
  copied from another portfolio.
- Build visual tension by contrasting dense art moments with quiet, readable
  sections for capabilities and case-study content.

## Page architecture

1. **Hero identity** — creator name/role, 1–2 sentence perspective, a signature
   visual, availability/contact signal, and light navigation.
2. **Manifesto and proof** — articulate how the creator thinks, then support it
   with honest counts, capabilities, tools, or selected client outcomes.
3. **Services** — show 3–6 offerings through original art cards and short,
   outcome-focused descriptions. Do not make cards decorative only.
4. **Selected work** — an image-led case-study index with role, scope, year,
   links, and a clear path to individual project pages.
5. **Process** — move from discovery through direction, making, launch, and
   iteration in the creator's real workflow. Explain what clients receive.
6. **Journal or experiments** — optional, but use real articles/experiments to
   show depth and keep the site alive.
7. **Closing contact** — a memorable final statement followed by a standard,
   low-friction inquiry form and direct email option.

## Interaction and motion

- Use a pinned or scroll-reactive hero only when it makes the visual concept more
  legible. Release the reader into normal content quickly.
- Animate type, masks, images, and small indicators with transforms and opacity.
  The site should still work as a clean portfolio with JavaScript disabled.
- Canvas/WebGL, particles, and custom cursors are optional embellishments. Do
  not sacrifice responsiveness, pointer accuracy, or battery life for them.
- Keep case-study hovers informative: reveal role, project category, or CTA; do
  not rely on an image-only interaction.
- Offer reduced motion and respect touch devices by removing hover-only behavior.

## Build guidance

- Treat the portfolio as evidence: show the creator's role, constraints,
  approach, outcomes, and credits for each project.
- Keep content independent from animation so SEO, screen readers, and mobile
  readers get the full narrative.
- Optimize visual assets, lazy-load rich effects, and avoid full-page video or
  WebGL on first paint.
- Ensure navigation, contact controls, keyboard focus, contrast, and form errors
  remain obvious against every high-contrast art direction.

## Delivery checklist

- The visual identity is unmistakably the new creator's, with original assets.
- The portfolio proves skill through cases, not just animation spectacle.
- Performance and accessibility are preserved on mobile and reduced-motion.
- Contact is clear, real, and never hidden behind a cinematic interaction.
