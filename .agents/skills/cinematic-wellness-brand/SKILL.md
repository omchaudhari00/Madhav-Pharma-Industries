# Cinematic Wellness Brand

Create a high-end, science-forward wellness experience that feels atmospheric,
intentional, and calm. The goal is a story of transformation—not a conventional
e-commerce landing page full of cards and calls to action.

## Originality boundary

Use the reference only for design principles. Never reuse its name, logo,
packaging geometry, green product art, Japanese copy, claims, interview assets,
or specific visual compositions. Request the new brand's own product imagery,
scientific substantiation, and legal review before making health claims.

## Visual direction

- Start nearly black: charcoal, deep mineral green, or another brand-specific
  low-light color. Reserve a single luminous accent for moments of energy.
- Use a large clean sans serif for display type. Let a few oversized words crop
  at the viewport edges; balance them with tiny, precise labels and chapter IDs.
- Use high-quality editorial photography, macro texture, or a restrained 3D
  atmosphere. Treat the product as a quiet artifact rather than a floating ad.
- Keep navigation light and sparse. A fixed mark, 4–5 destinations, a menu, and
  one final purchase/contact route are enough.
- Build long empty areas into the composition. Silence and scale should make the
  product feel more considered, not less informative.

## Page architecture

1. **Atmospheric hero** — short positioning line, a large interrupted display
   phrase, a symbolic product or environmental visual, and a subtle scroll cue.
2. **Core belief** — introduce a named idea or promise, then frame it in 3–5
   numbered chapters that move from problem to mechanism to outcome.
3. **Science / proof** — explain ingredients, methods, testing, or expertise in
   plain language. Link evidence; distinguish research from marketing claims.
4. **Product reveal** — show the product within a real-life context, followed by
   concise specifications, usage, and a clear primary action.
5. **Human proof** — use original experts, creators, customer stories, or case
   studies as a visual story index rather than an anonymous testimonial wall.
6. **Use moments** — contrast two or more states such as work/rest, day/night,
   or focus/recovery. Use image-led panels with short supporting copy.
7. **Conversion and footer** — repeat the product promise, show the CTA, then
   provide legal, privacy, and scientific-disclosure routes.

## Interaction and motion

- Let each chapter occupy at least one deliberate screenful. Use pinning only
  when it reveals a genuine step in the story.
- Reveal type, imagery, and graphic marks through opacity, translation, clips,
  or masked crops. Do not animate layout dimensions during scroll.
- Use a hero canvas or WebGL only when it adds a clear atmospheric effect. A
  layered image, CSS gradients, and video fallback are usually more robust.
- Keep motion quiet: 250–600 ms for UI changes and slow scroll-linked reveals
  for the narrative. Avoid perpetual decorative loops.
- Supply `prefers-reduced-motion` with static imagery, readable text, and no
  scroll-jacking. All navigation and CTA paths must work without animation.

## Build guidance

- Use semantic sections, headings, descriptive image alternatives, keyboard
  navigation, and visible focus states even if the visual system is minimal.
- Load hero media responsively; use AVIF/WebP and a poster frame for video.
- Use a content model for chapters, studies, story cards, and use moments so the
  site can be updated without rewriting the interface.
- Validate contrast over imagery, especially in the hero and fixed navigation.

## Delivery checklist

- The story has one coherent message and one brand-specific visual metaphor.
- Science copy is sourced, careful, and does not promise unproven outcomes.
- The design still feels premium when animation is turned off.
- The brand, photography, packaging, colors, and copy are original to the new
  project—not a close reconstruction of the source inspiration.
