# Luxury Residence Storytelling

Create a premium property experience that moves from atmosphere to proof to
inquiry. It should feel editorial and architectural—never like a generic listing
website or a wall of luxury clichés.

## Originality boundary

Do not reuse the project name, monogram, visual identity, tower renders,
location, amenities, floor plans, numbers, map content, developer identity, or
sales copy from the source. Use only approved property details and licensed
imagery. Clearly label future, conceptual, or unavailable features.

## Visual direction

- Lead with a cinematic original exterior or interior render at dusk, dawn, or
  another brand-appropriate time. Use an unobtrusive dark overlay for text.
- Build a palette from the property: warm stone, champagne, terracotta, deep
  graphite, soft cream, or another material-led set.
- Pair a high-contrast editorial serif for property name and large moments with
  a narrow, clean sans for coordinates, navigation, and technical facts.
- Use restrained all-caps labels, generous tracking, thin rules, and sparse
  fixed UI. The brand should feel confident without visual noise.

## Page architecture

1. **Full-viewport arrival** — property name, concise positioning, developer
   mark, and fixed access points for inquiry, contact, and navigation.
2. **The project** — narrative on setting, audience, and defining idea. Replace
   vague superlatives with concrete, approved qualities.
3. **Location** — accessible map, transport/drive/walk controls, real nearby
   destinations, and transparent travel-time sourcing.
4. **Architecture** — concept, verified facts, facade/material details, image
   sequence, and an accessible gallery with captions.
5. **Landscape and amenities** — separate public realm, private grounds, and
   service lifestyle into clear visual modules; add plans only when readable.
6. **Interiors** — design story, material palette, lighting, arrival sequence,
   and real amenities. Use galleries with text alternatives and controls.
7. **Residences** — inventory or representative layouts, bedroom filters,
   area/ceiling information, availability status, and accurate disclaimers.
8. **Inquiry** — simple lead form, sales contact, privacy consent, and legal
   disclosures. Provide a human contact path as well as the form.

## Interaction and motion

- Keep core contact and navigation fixed but compact. They must remain visible
  over light and dark imagery with sufficient contrast.
- Use slow crossfades, image reveals, subtle parallax, and marquee labels to
  establish pace. Do not hide essential property information in animation.
- Galleries, maps, residence selectors, and 3D tours must have conventional
  controls, keyboard support, no-autoplay fallback, and clear close behavior.
- A 3D tour should be optional, lazy-loaded, and never block the floor-plan or
  inquiry route. Show a static accessible preview when WebGL is unavailable.
- Respect reduced motion; turn scroll scenes into normal static sections.

## Build guidance

- Separate factual content from presentation: property details, floor plans,
  gallery media, locations, availability, and disclosures should be data-driven.
- For a real development, run all copy, distances, floor areas, and marketing
  claims through the relevant legal and sales approval process.
- Optimize large visual media aggressively. Load the hero poster first and defer
  galleries, maps, tours, and video until needed.
- Use semantic regions, meaningful labels, form validation, accessible modal
  dialogs, and visible focus states throughout.

## Delivery checklist

- The property has its own material palette and story—not borrowed branding.
- Major facts, maps, amenities, layouts, and availability are true or labelled
  clearly as illustrative.
- Navigation, inquiry, gallery, and residence discovery work without animation.
- The mobile experience keeps visual drama while prioritizing clear information
  and contact access.
